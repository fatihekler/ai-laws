# AI-LAWS — START HERE

AI-LAWS is a research, evidence, legal-engineering and accountability corpus for artificial-intelligence law, liability, rights, remedies, incident evidence, financial responsibility and cross-project legal interfaces.

## Project purpose

The repository exists to answer, with source-backed discipline:

> When an AI system, model, agent, provider, deployer, operator or AI-enabled institution causes or materially contributes to a risk, incident, rights impact or harm, what legal rules may apply; what evidence is required; who may bear responsibility; what remedies or financial-responsibility mechanisms exist; what remains uncertain; and which non-legal project must consume the finding?

AI-LAWS is not a court, regulator or law firm and does not create legal advice, guilt, liability or binding precedent.

## Main research layers

- jurisdiction and applicable-law mapping;
- primary legislation and treaty obligations;
- administrative/regulatory duties;
- case law and analogical precedents;
- fundamental/human rights and human sovereignty;
- AI actor and organizational responsibility;
- causation, foreseeability, standard of care and evidence;
- incident reporting, logging and preservation;
- remedies, compensation and sanctions;
- insurance, catastrophe finance and financial responsibility;
- state/national-security accountability gaps;
- cross-border and conflict-of-laws questions;
- cross-repo routing to OWASP, Ethical-AI, Engineering OS, Esmaul Husna and Apesteori.

## Research-assistant boundary

ChatGPT, Grok, YargıGPT, NotebookLM/Gemini Notebook and other tools are research assistants only. Their outputs remain non-canonical until original sources are verified and repository-local review accepts the exact claim.
