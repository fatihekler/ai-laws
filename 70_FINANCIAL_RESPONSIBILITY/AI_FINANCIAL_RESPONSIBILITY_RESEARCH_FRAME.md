# AI-LAWS — AI FINANCIAL RESPONSIBILITY RESEARCH FRAME

## Purpose

Research whether and how AI-related harms can be financed, insured, compensated or internalized. This file distinguishes existing mechanisms from proposals.

## Core problem

A legal duty without an economically viable compensation mechanism may leave victims without effective redress, especially where catastrophic or correlated losses exceed the responsible entity's assets or available insurance.

This is a research problem, not a conclusion that current law universally requires special AI funds.

## Research layers

### Existing insurance

Research:
- cyber insurance;
- technology errors and omissions;
- product liability;
- professional indemnity;
- directors/officers insurance;
- general liability;
- specialty AI endorsements/exclusions;
- government indemnities where relevant.

For each jurisdiction/product, verify actual wording and exclusions rather than inferring coverage from category name.

### Mandatory financial assurance

Research whether current law requires:
- mandatory insurance;
- minimum capital/reserves;
- bonding;
- guarantees;
- compensation schemes;
- sector pools;
- operator financial responsibility.

Relevant analogies may include nuclear, environmental, transport, financial services or other high-risk regimes, but analogy does not establish AI applicability.

### Catastrophic-loss mechanisms

Research as current law or proposals, clearly separated:
- catastrophe bonds;
- industry mutual pools;
- public-private reinsurance;
- victim compensation funds;
- developer/operator reserve requirements;
- layered liability caps/floors;
- mandatory first-party compensation;
- state backstops.

### Judgment-proof / insolvency risk

Research:
- when expected harm may exceed firm assets;
- corporate-structure/ring-fencing issues;
- insolvency priority;
- parent/subsidiary liability;
- mandatory capitalization arguments;
- insurance market capacity;
- moral hazard and adverse selection.

## Initial research questions

1. Which existing policies plausibly cover AI-caused losses and what exclusions matter?
2. Are any jurisdictions imposing AI-specific insurance or financial-assurance duties?
3. What high-risk industry analogies are legally transferable, and where do they fail?
4. How would correlated, cross-border, delayed or latent AI losses affect insurability?
5. Who contributes to a pooled fund: model developer, deployer, compute provider, high-risk operator, or another class?
6. What prevents financial assurance from becoming an entry barrier that only incumbents can satisfy?
7. How should compensation interact with fault, strict liability, regulatory fines and private claims?
