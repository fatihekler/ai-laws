# AI-LAWS — PROJECT CHARTER

## Mission

AI-LAWS builds a source-verifiable legal and accountability corpus for artificial-intelligence systems, agents, providers, deployers, operators, institutions and affected persons across jurisdictions.

The project is designed to connect law with evidence, engineering, security, human rights, economics and institutional responsibility without collapsing those disciplines into one another.

## Primary questions

1. What legal norm, treaty obligation, regulatory duty, precedent or principle may apply?
2. What facts and evidence are required before asserting breach, causation, liability or remedy?
3. Which actor had which role, knowledge, authority and duty?
4. What rights or protected interests may be affected?
5. What remedies, compensation, sanctions or injunctive powers exist?
6. What financial-responsibility mechanisms exist or are only proposals?
7. What jurisdictional, sovereign-immunity, national-security or cross-border gaps remain?
8. What technical/security evidence must another project produce?
9. What remains UNKNOWN or requires a qualified human lawyer, regulator, judge, domain expert or policy decision?

## Scope

Research may cover:

- constitutional and human-rights law;
- civil/tort/contract/product liability;
- criminal law and cybercrime;
- administrative/public law;
- consumer law;
- privacy/data-protection/biometric/neurodata law;
- employment/labour law;
- competition/antitrust and market power;
- platform/intermediary law;
- intellectual property and trade secrets;
- cybersecurity obligations;
- financial, insurance and catastrophe-liability law;
- health/medical and professional liability;
- children's rights and vulnerable persons;
- national-security, intelligence, defence and state accountability;
- international law, treaty law and conflict of laws;
- evidence, procedure, discovery, logging and record preservation;
- AI-specific statutes, regulations, codes and standards;
- remedies, redress, compensation and sanctions;
- governance, audit and independent oversight.

## Non-goals

AI-LAWS does not:

- issue legal advice;
- declare a person/company guilty or liable;
- fabricate precedent;
- treat an allegation as fact;
- treat ethical/theological claims as positive law;
- treat technical scanner output as legal proof;
- treat a policy preference as existing law;
- replace qualified local counsel, courts, regulators or required human review;
- provide operational instructions for harmful conduct.

## Project outputs

- jurisdiction profiles;
- source registries;
- legal concept taxonomies;
- case-law corpus records;
- incident-to-law analysis records;
- liability/causation/remedy matrices;
- rights and human-sovereignty analyses;
- financial-responsibility research;
- cross-border comparison matrices;
- gaps/conflicts/unknown registers;
- policy proposals clearly separated from current law;
- cross-repo evidence interfaces;
- review and handoff packages.

## Fundamental project principle

The repository should make it harder to say either:

> “There is definitely liability because an AI said so.”

or:

> “There is no rule because no AI-specific statute was found.”

The correct task is to identify the applicable legal architecture, evidence and uncertainty with source-level discipline.
