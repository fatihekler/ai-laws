# AI-LAWS — HUMAN SOVEREIGNTY, COGNITIVE LIBERTY AND DIGNITY RESEARCH FRAME

## Purpose

This file defines a legal-research frame for human dignity, agency, mental autonomy, cognitive liberty, privacy, appeal and reversibility in AI-mediated environments.

It is not a declaration that every jurisdiction recognizes every right named below.

## Core research proposition

AI safety is not limited to preventing technical compromise. Legal research must also ask whether AI-mediated systems materially affect a person's ability to remain an autonomous rights-bearing subject rather than an object of profiling, manipulation or irreversible automated action.

## Research dimensions

### Human dignity

Research constitutional, human-rights, civil-law and sectoral protections against degrading, objectifying or dehumanizing treatment.

### Decision autonomy and agency

Research:
- right to meaningful human involvement where recognized;
- automated-decision safeguards;
- ability to refuse/opt out;
- human override;
- appeal/review;
- informed choice;
- coercion and dark-pattern/manipulation rules.

### Mental privacy / cognitive liberty

Research:
- neural data;
- inferred mental/emotional states;
- biometric and behavioural inference;
- neurotechnology;
- brain-computer interfaces;
- voice/face/physiological inference;
- profiling and prediction;
- legal protection of thought, conscience and mental integrity.

Do not assume `MENTAL_PRIVACY` is a named legal right in every jurisdiction.

### AI-mediated dependency and influence

Research legal relevance of:
- dependency-inducing design;
- authority projection;
- parasocial attachment;
- deceptive anthropomorphism;
- targeted persuasion;
- exploitation of vulnerability;
- political or commercial manipulation;
- children's vulnerability;
- inability to distinguish human/machine interaction.

### Reversibility and contestability

Research whether affected persons can:
- know an automated decision occurred;
- understand material reasons where legally required;
- challenge the decision;
- obtain human review;
- correct underlying data;
- stop ongoing processing;
- reverse or mitigate irreversible consequences.

## Human-sovereignty invariants as research questions

The following are normative research questions, not universal legal rules:

```text
CAN_THE_PERSON_REFUSE_AI_MEDIATION?
CAN_THE_PERSON_IDENTIFY_AI_INVOLVEMENT?
CAN_THE_PERSON_CHALLENGE_THE_OUTCOME?
CAN_A_HUMAN_REVIEWER_OVERRIDE_THE_SYSTEM?
IS_THE_ACTION_REVERSIBLE?
IS_MENTAL_OR_BIOMETRIC_INFERENCE_DISCLOSED_AND_LAWFUL?
IS_THE_PERSON_BEING_MANIPULATED_THROUGH_KNOWN_VULNERABILITIES?
IS_RESPONSIBILITY_TRACEABLE_TO_HUMAN_OR_INSTITUTIONAL_ACTORS?
```

## No moral-scoring rule

Do not build a system that scores a person's dignity, faith, morality, intention or human worth.

```text
HUMAN_DIGNITY_PROTECTION != HUMAN_WORTH_SCORING
MENTAL_AUTONOMY_PROTECTION != THOUGHT_POLICING
```
