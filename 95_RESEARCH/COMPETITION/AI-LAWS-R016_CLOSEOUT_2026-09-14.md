## Material results

- EU Article 101 supplies the agreement/association-decision/concerted-practice competition baseline; R016 does not infer agreement from parallel public AI-safety statements.
- 2023 EU Horizontal Cooperation Guidelines provide a nonbinding framework for information exchange and standardisation. Safety/technical cooperation remains context-dependent; transparency, necessity, participation and access can matter, but there is no universal safe harbor.
- EU Article 102 requires a dominance/abuse analysis; concentration or oligopoly alone is not treated as abuse.
- EU Merger Regulation analysis requires a concentration/control and jurisdiction/effect inquiry; partnership is not automatically concentration or unlawful.
- DMA contestability/fairness duties are relevant only where its gatekeeper/core-platform-service scope is met.
- U.S. Sherman Act §§ 1–2, Clayton Act §7 and FTC Act §5 were current-source marker-verified through the Office of Law Revision Counsel.
- Current 2023 Merger Guidelines remain nonbinding enforcement guidance and expressly create no independent rights or obligations.
- The 2014 cybersecurity information-sharing statement remains usable only as a narrow analogy; it does not create an AI-safety collaboration safe harbor.
- FTC's AI partnership 6(b) report identifies areas to watch but is not an adjudication.
- The 2000 competitor-collaboration guidelines were withdrawn in December 2024; the 2026 public inquiry is not final replacement guidance.
